What is GetComput?

GetComput is a statistics learning website, in Indonesian and English. The material is arranged in order from the basics to advanced methods, and each topic has a calculator so you can practise with your own data. You choose a method, enter data, and get result tables, charts, assumption tests, and an automatic interpretation that can be downloaded to Excel.

Who is it for?

Students, researchers, and practitioners who analyse experimental data (agriculture, animal science, fisheries, forestry, health, industry) or observational data, without writing code.

How are the calculations done?

Calculations use Python scientific libraries (NumPy and SciPy). Analysis of variance for experimental designs uses sequential (Type I) sums of squares with Indonesian reporting conventions: the marks **, *, and tn (not significant), the coefficient of variation (KK), and letter notation for post-hoc tests. The type of sum of squares, reference categories, and estimation method may differ from other software; verification is still recommended for publication.

How to read the results

TermMeaning
** / * / tnHighly significant (P<0.01) / significant (P<0.05) / not significant.
KKCoefficient of variation (KK) = √error MS ÷ grand mean × 100%. The smaller it is, the more precise the experiment.
Letter notationMeans followed by the same letter do not differ significantly in the chosen post-hoc test.
R², R² adjProportion of the variation in Y explained by the model (adj = adjusted for the number of variables).
VIFA multicollinearity measure; > 10 signals a serious problem.
AIC / BICModel selection criteria; smaller is better.
Odds ratio / IRR / HRMultiplier of odds (logistic), event rate (Poisson), and hazard (Cox) for a 1-unit increase in X.

Methodology notes

  • Analysis of variance uses sequential (Type I) sums of squares; on balanced data this equals Type II/III.
  • Assumption tests are supportive only; small samples make them underpowered, so also look at the residual plots.
  • Post-hoc tests should be used only when the F test is significant. LSD (BNT) does not control type I error across many comparisons; Tukey and Scheffé are more conservative; Dunnett is for comparisons against a control.
  • Advanced models (panel, spatial, mixed, Cox, Tobit) are analysis aids; check the model assumptions and consult a statistician for publication.
